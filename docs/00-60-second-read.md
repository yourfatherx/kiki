**Kiki**
Kiki turns everyday sound into shapes that deaf and hard-of-hearing people can read at a glance.

*Product & visual design · Concept, 2026 · Aurvind Mohanty*

### The one rule
**Sharp sound, spiky shape. Soft sound, round shape.**
It's borrowed from the kiki–bouba effect: across languages, people match spiky shapes to sharp sounds. Every sound in Kiki, from running water to a smoke alarm, sits somewhere on that scale.

### Three moments
1. **The alarm pulses like the alarm.** Three beats, then a pause: the real smoke-alarm rhythm. The motion carries meaning, not decoration.
2. **Tone becomes type.** A shout is heavy and sharp; a whisper is light and soft. Your name always stands out.
3. **Ding, then dong.** Two rings at two heights, rippling out like a bell. Readable from across the room.

### How I work
- 1 rule → 12 sound shapes
- Colour is never the only signal
- No flashing faster than 3× a second
- Audio never leaves the phone

A concept project. The next step is testing the kiki–bouba mapping with deaf participants before trusting it.
